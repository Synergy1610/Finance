### Central Banks
Basically control the money in a country (RBI).
Responsible for controlling the economy through monetary policies. **Monetary Policy** is controlling the amount of money in the economy and the intrest rates.

Scenario 1: **If the economy is moving slowly**, then they try to boost the economy by *expansionary monetary policies*, where intrest rates reduce, more money goes in circulation either by printing more or market techniquies like [[Open Market Operations|quantitative easing]].

Scenario 2: **If there is too much money in circulation**, this leads to *infaltion*, where the value of the currency decreases. This causes the prices of items to increase. Then the Central Banks use *contractionary monetary policies* where they increase intrest rates, and reduce cash flow by [[Open Market Operations|quantitative tightening]].

### Commercial Bank
Where most people's money lives (SBI, ICICI).
But their main role is to act as a *medium between people with surplus money and those in need of money*.
When people/companies come and deposit money. The banks then uses this money and lends it to people/companies that are in need of it through *loans* etc.

Key Insight: Banks actually create money, though **Freactional Reserve Banking**, a 100rs deposit can create 1000rs in the economy.

But they dont just lend out how much ever money someone has deposited. The Central Bank sets something called a *CRR* which is Cash Reserve Ratio, If that is lets say 4%, then the bank can only loan out 96% of the deposits. 

*Higher CRR = Less lending = Less money in economy*

**Note:** Commercial Banks urge people to deposit money, though giving them payout on intrest like (3-4%), and they charge 8-12% intrest on loans. And this is how they make profits.

### Pension Funds
They take a percentage of our paycheck even before it reaches our bank account. They then invest this money in a diverse portfolio (Stocks, Real Estate, Bonds etc).
And they *pay us back with returns after we retire*. 

Its not just our money, but our employer also contributes a matching amount on top of ours.
This exists because most people dont save enough for retirement on their own, so this system makes it mandatory and automatic, and we *never see the money so we never spend it*.

**How they work:** Pension fund collects your money → invests in stocks, bonds, govt securities → those generate returns → passed back to you as "interest". It's really your share of investment profits, not interest out of nowhere

**Note:** The interest here is [[Interest|compound interest]].You earn returns on your original amount *plus* all previously earned returns. Grows exponentially over time. Time is the biggest factor — starting early makes a massive difference.

*They charge us a tiny mangement fee.*

### Mutual Funds
Think of it as a club managed by professionals, where many people pool their money and the professionals invest it in a diverse portfolio (Stocks, Bonds, Gold etc).

We own *units* of the fund. The price of one unit is called **NAV** (Net Asset Value), When investments do well NAV goes up. When they perform poorly, NAV goes down.

*Small management fee charged called the expense ratio*, Everything else is ours.

**Why it exists:** Buying individual stocks requires knowledge, time, and large capital. Mutual funds let anyone — even with a small amount per month — access a diversified portfolio managed by professionals.

### Hedge Funds
Are similar to mutual funds, where a pool of money is managaed by professionals. But for the *ultra rich only*, the minimum investment is typically in the millions.

**Why only the rich:** They're largely unregulated compared to mutual funds. Governments allow this because wealthy investors are assumed to understand and absorb the risk.

**The Key Difference:** Mutual funds mostly buy assests hoping for them to go up. But Hedge funds use aggressive strategies like 
- Leverage: borrowing money to invest more than they actually have, amplifying gains and losses.
- Short Selling: Betting that a stock will go down
and many such strategies. Basically tryna make money however the market changes.

**What they charge:** It is called 2 and 20, so they charge 2% management fees and also take 20% of the profits.

### Investment Banks
Basically provide money to big companies, governments and corporations.
They do a lot of tasks but their main three are:
- IPO
- Mergers and Acquistions
- Raising Capital

**IPO**
When a private company wants to go public and offer their stocks to the public, they contact such investment banks that go through documents and details and find how much the company is worth and hype it up to investors. This is called an IPO (Initial Public Offering).

**Mergers and Acquitions**
When one company wants to merge with or buy another company, then the investment banks are the ones handling everything and making sure nothing goes wrong.

**Raising Capital**
When companies need more money than what they can get from a normal bank loan, thats when they reach out to investment banks.

### Private Equity Firms
They raise funds from ultra rich individuals, pension funds, hedge funds and buy private companies and then upgrade (Flip) them and then sell them later for more profit.

**How they actually "upgrade" a company:**
- Cut costs aggressively
- Replace management
- Restructure operations
- Sometimes break it into parts and sell each part separately

**How they exit (make their profit):**
- IPO — take the company public
- Sell to another company (M&A)
- Sell to another private equity firm

### Insurance Companies
They **take in premiums from customers and in return promise to pay if something bad happens** (Car crash, Medical Bills etc).
Chances of something bad happening to everyone at the same time is very low, so these companies just use the money of one person to pay another who needs. And they still have a lot of money left over as the keep collecting premiums, which they invest in bonds, stocks, real estate etc. 

### Venture Capital 
These companies **make bets by investing in startups and hope they blow up and make them profits**. They usually collect money from rich people, pension funds and hedge funds. And invest in small up and coming companies. And they usually invest in many different companies, because most of them flop, but the ones that hit , hit it big and make so much profit that it covers all the loses.
