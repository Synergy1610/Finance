#finance 

**Commonly Used Jargons:** [Varsity Page](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/commonly-used-jargons/)

*Note:* The stock market in India is open from 9:15 AM and 3:30 PM IST.

**NIFTY:** It is basically the weighted average of the top 50 stocks in NSE. Therefore it is a good way to find out what the returns of equity are.

**SENSEX:** It is similar to NIFTY, but for the top 30 stocks of BSE.

**LTP:** Last Traded Price

**Securites:** They are tradeable financial assets. ex: Equity Securites

**Bullish Market:** In a bullish market, prices tend to go up.

**Bearish Market:** In a bearish market, prices tend to go down.

**Derivative:** You are buying a financial contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset. So we are basically buying a contract that bets on the price movement of the asset. This includes Futures and Options.

### Path when a stock is bought

Reference: [SEBI on Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/regulators/), [All the others](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/financial-intermediaries/)

**Stock Broker:** The middle man that connects us to the stock exchange, in my case it is ICICI Direct. The Account we have at broker is called the Trading Account.

**Exchange:** It is the marketplace where buy and sell orders of the stocks are matched. India has NSE and BSE.

**Clearing Corporations:** They make sure the trades are done completely, that is making sure that that money is debited and the shares are credited.

**Depository:** This is where our shares will get credited, which we can then view in our broker terminal.

**SEBI:** Securities and Exchange Board of India. They are the regulators of the entirity of India's Stock Market and make sure that everything runs smoothly.

### Corporate Actions

Reference: [Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/five-corporate-actions-and-its-impact-on-stock-prices/)

These are the ways companies reward share holders.

**Dividends:** The company decides to share a portion of their profits with their shareholders. They are distributed on a pershare basis.

**Bonus:** Free shares that shareholders get from the comapny reserve.
But the net value doesnt change, as the share value reduces.
Companies issue bonus shares to encourage retail participation, especially when the company’s price per share is very high, and it becomes tough for new investors to buy shares. The number of outstanding shares increases by issuing bonus shares, but the share price is slashed.

**Stock Split:** The face value of the stock decreases and the number of shares increases depending on a ratio.

**Buybacks:** When the company decides to buy its own shares from the market usually at a premium price. Done to reduce the free market shares.
